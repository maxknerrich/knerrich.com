# Your Factory is a **Garden**

Software factories are all the hype right now. Everybody on Twitter posts highly complex architecture diagrams of their factories, while at the same time companies like [Warp](https://warp.dev) shift their whole business model to them.

I do agree that the future of software development is some sort of self-healing software. But I don't think autonomous factories are the right mental model for it. They promote a set-it-and-forget-it idea, which quickly turns into a slop factory.

A better mental model is that of a garden, with the developer as the gardener. _A factory runs without you, a garden goes wild without you._ The software grows on its own and heals minor issues, but only because we as developers keep tending it: pulling weeds (removing slop), planting new seeds (system architecture) and providing fertilizer (letting agents verify their own work).

## Agents are better at Programming

While it might hurt to admit for everybody who enjoyed the craft of writing code the past decades, agents are at this point better programmers then 99.9% of us, tendency imprving as well.

While even a few months ago, GPT-5.5 was vomiting phyton looking TypeScript code all over the codbase, current gen models, especially Opus-5.5 is actually producing very good looking and especially more imprtant, very functional code.
