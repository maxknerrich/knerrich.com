# Content

Projects and writing are Markdown in `src/content/`; the name is the URL. A project is a folder, `src/content/projects/<slug>/index.md`, with its images beside it. A piece of writing is a file, `src/content/writing/<slug>.md`, or a folder like a project's, with its images beside it. Each starts with a `#` title, an optional bullet list of tags, and an opening paragraph that doubles as the page description (writing can set `description` in its frontmatter instead; it's what lists and the feed show). The title is plain text. To start one, copy `TEMPLATE.md` in the same folder to `<slug>/index.md` (for writing, `<slug>.md` works too); the templates themselves are never published.

**Projects** start with frontmatter: `year` (2025, or a span like 2023–2025), `industry`, `color` (the client's brand color as `#rrggbb`, which tints the case study: its glow and the lit bars of its charts) and an optional `website`, linked from the case study. `draft: true` keeps the project to `vp dev`: every build leaves it out, card and case study. After the title comes a list of the services (the work done), the description, then the cover: `./cover.jpg`, 1920×1200, made on the "Case Studys" page of the Web file in Figma. It's the image over the case study's title and the preview image, and the card's image unless there's a `./card.jpg` beside it: 5:6, at least 900px wide, made for the near-square landing card, which crops a cover's sides. Keep a card's subject in the middle; the tags sit over its top and the title over its bottom. Let every image breathe: one subject, whole and scaled down on the project's own backdrop (a card's subject at most about 60% of its width and half its height, a cover's at most about 70%, a screenshot in the write-up with a tenth of margin on each side), never cropped edge to edge. Photos are the exception. The paragraphs right after the cover sit next to the services; the rest is the write-up. Projects are listed most recent first.

**Images** beside a piece are linked by their path from it, `![Alt](./photo.jpg)`, and the build serves small, modern versions of them. Other files, like a PDF, go in `static/` and are linked from `/`. A paragraph of only images, two or more (one per line is fine), is a gallery: a row of small images that scrolls sideways, each labelled with its title, `![Alt](./toolbar.jpg "Toolbar")`. A `_caption_` line after it captions the whole row.

**Embeds** let a piece show a live component instead of a picture: a code block named after the component, with its props as YAML (see `src/lib/embeds/`).

````md
```compare
before: ./before.jpg
after: ./after.jpg
alt: What the two images show
```
````

**TypeScript code blocks** (` ```ts `) are type-checked like the site's code, with errors shown in the Markdown. Each block has its own scope and can import from the project (`#lib/…`) or its packages. Mark a block ` ```ts nocheck ` for a fragment or pseudo-code.

**In the text:**

- Notes: `> [!NOTE] Optional title` as a quote's first line (also `TIP`, `IMPORTANT`, `WARNING`, `CAUTION`); the rest of the quote is the note.
- Code: ` ```ts title="review.ts" `, highlighted at build, with line numbers and a copy button. `title` is the only option; languages are `CODE_LANGS` in `src/lib/writing/syntax.ts`, and anything else fails the build.
- Keys: `<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd>`.
- Footnotes: `[^id]` in the text, `[^id]: The note.` anywhere below.
- External links open in a new tab.
- Blocks: `compare` (before/after images, as wide as the page's images), `tweet` (`id: "…"`, fetched at build), `stats` (up to four headline numbers), `chart` (bars for one or two series, with a table of the numbers; the last series is what the chart is about, in the page's brand color, and the one before it is context, in grey; a value can be a range `[low, high]`), `cards` (short points as a bento of tiles: `title`, an optional `icon` from `src/lib/icons/glyphs.ts`, `text` that opens on hover or focus where there's a pointer to hover with, and `wide` for a tile two columns across; to cite a source, put the footnote in the text around it, since footnotes don't work inside a block), `timing` (two durations per row, acted out at real speed in two small app windows, client-server and local-first: a row's `action` is `open` (a spinner, then the list; `issues` sets the count), `create` or `update` (a pending spinner on the issue); the rows are choices in a floating panel, and `marker` lights a time under it), `paper` (a paper's title page on a stack of sheets, opening the PDF: `href`, `page` as `./<file>.svg` beside the piece, `title` and optional `pages`; make the SVG from the PDF's first page with PyMuPDF's `page.get_svg_image(text_as_path=True)`), `video` (a short muted clip that loops while it's on screen: `src` as `./<file>.mp4` beside the piece, `poster` as its first frame, `alt` and an optional `caption`; keep clips to a few seconds and a couple of MB, H.264 with `-movflags +faststart`), and the visualizations below. Their props are in `src/lib/embeds/props.ts`.

Mistakes in any of these fail the build, and show in the editor while writing. `src/content/writing/components/` (a seed) uses them all.

### Visualizations

`sync-steps` steps one issue through the thesis's sync algorithm, from a local edit and one on GitHub to both merged, and takes an `id`; link to a step with `[step 2](step:<id>/2)`, and `autoplay: true` steps through on its own (not with reduced motion). `sync-live` plays the architecture: writes logged beside the app's data, then synced to the API and back; `loop: false` plays it once. They belong to the thesis case study and live beside it, in `src/content/projects/local-first-api-sync/`, but any piece can use them. `requests-live` compares the requests a client-server and a local-first app send for the same edits, with sliders for edits per minute and the sync interval. The Brunkhorst case study has two of its own, in `src/content/projects/brunkhorst/`: `car-data-live` plays a car page lighting up field by field next to the structured data it carries, and `agent-search-steps` steps through a browser agent searching the cars with WebMCP (it takes `id` and `autoplay` like `sync-steps`). The tldraw case study's `kit-steps`, in `src/content/projects/tldraw/`, steps a tile from flat to soft isometric, the way each 3D part was made (also `id` and `autoplay`).

A new one is a Svelte component built from the shells: `Stepper` (`src/lib/Stepper.svelte`) draws a step from its index and needs a `label`, the drawing's fixed name for screen readers, kept beside its `steps`, and `Playback` (`src/lib/Playback.svelte`) plays, pauses and restarts whatever it animates with `element.animate()`. Draw with the palette, `viz` in `src/lib/styles/stepper.ts`, and keep a step's whole state in one row of a table, as `steps` in `sync-drawing.ts` does, so the drawing only shows a state. Give it a block: its props in `src/lib/embeds/props.ts`, its component in `src/lib/embeds/index.ts`, and, if step links can point at it, its name in `VISUALIZATIONS` and its step count in `STEP_COUNTS` (`src/lib/server/markdown/embeds.ts`), so the build rejects a link past the last step.

**Writing is a digital garden**: pieces are published early and grow. The frontmatter says how far along a piece is (`stage`: seed, seedling, budding, evergreen; `seedling` if unset) and when it was first published and last reworked (`created`, `updated`). Type `created` once, when the piece is first published; every piece but a seed needs it. Bump `updated` by hand only for real changes, not typos, so "last tended" means something to readers; without it, it's `created`, and it can't be before `created`. A `seed` is an idea, not published yet: it only shows in `vp dev`. Every build, preview deployments included, leaves seeds and draft projects out, with their images, videos and components, so a preview shows what `main` will publish.

The feed, `/rss.xml`, is Atom rather than RSS 2.0 because Atom gives each entry both a published and an updated date: `created` and `updated`, so feed readers show reworked pieces as updated. The feed's own date is its latest entry's `updated`, and a seed without dates stays out of it. Entries carry the description and link to the piece on the site.

**Preview images** (what a link shows when shared) are made at build: the title on graph paper for writing, the lead image with the Knerrich sticker for projects. To use your own instead, put it at `src/content/og/<page>.jpg` or `.png`, where `<page>` is the URL path, e.g. `src/content/og/writing/my-piece.jpg`, or as `og.jpg` in a piece's or project's folder (1200×630; it's cropped to fit).

### Rendering

`src/lib/server/markdown/` renders Markdown into blocks for `<Prose>`, one module per kind of block (footnotes, notes, figures, embeds, code, headings, and keys and links in `inline.ts`), joined in `index.ts`. `<Prose>` shows them with `{@html}`. That's only safe because the HTML comes from this repository's Markdown: never pass it visitor input. StyleX can't style the children of `{@html}` by selector, so the renderer puts the classes on each element itself: by tag and `data-role` from `src/lib/styles/prose.ts`, and per code line from `src/lib/styles/code.ts` (`src/lib/server/markdown/code.ts`). Restyle prose there.
